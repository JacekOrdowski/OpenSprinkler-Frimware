# Crowd runner / gate runner games: how they really work, and what a children's math game can reuse

Research date: 2026-10-20. Many sources are store listings, vendor blogs and third-party analytics estimates. Reliability notes are given inline. Revenue and download figures are estimates and often conflict between sources.

## 1. Last War: Survival and similar 4X games: the real game vs the gate-runner ads, and the regulatory and press record

### Takeaway
Last War: Survival is a 4X base-building and alliance-war strategy game. The "pick the +/× door and grow your squad" runner in its ads is at most a small side mode or tutorial, and press describe the ads as having "practically nothing in common" with the real game. The developer turned the controversy into marketing ("developers made a real game based off the fake game in the ads"). I found no UK ASA ruling against Last War itself. The closest precedents are ASA rulings against Evony (2024, a "shooting numbered barrels" ad) and Playrix's Homescapes/Gardenscapes (2020, pin-pulling ads). In both, an ad showing a mini-game that exists in the game but is not the core experience was ruled misleading.

### Cited Findings
**What Last War actually is**
- Last War: Survival is a multiplayer mobile strategy game set in a post-apocalyptic zombie world, built around base building, hero recruitment, resource management and player-led alliances. It was released in August 2023 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_War:_Survival_Game)
- Developer: First Fun HK Ltd; development and publishing later moved to FUNFLY PTE. LTD. (Singapore, incorporated February 2024). The same team made Top War: Battle Game (about $1.1B lifetime revenue). Tencent holds about 10.71% of the original developer, Beijing Yuanqu Entertainment — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_War:_Survival_Game). Conflict: the aggregator site fakeadgames.com calls First Fun "a subsidiary of Century Games" — [Fake Ad Games](https://fakeadgames.com/archive/last-war-survival). This looks wrong (Century Games publishes Whiteout Survival). Prefer the Wikipedia version.
- Revenue: $287k in the launch month (August 2023); monthly revenue grew from $30M (January 2024) to $138M (December 2024); lifetime revenue passed $2B by February 2025; among the top five highest-grossing mobile games of 2024 — [Wikipedia, citing PocketGamer.biz 18 Feb 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_War:_Survival_Game)

**Regulatory precedents (UK ASA) and platform rules**
- ASA vs Top Games, "Evony: The King's Return" (reported 23 Apr 2024): an X video ad showed a character shooting numbered barrels rolling toward it ("Navigate obstacles, unleash devastating attacks"). The ASA found the core gameplay was city-building with puzzle, PvP and PvE elements. Players who skipped the other parts could not progress and would eventually lose access to the puzzles. The ad was ruled misleading under CAP Code rule 3.1. Top Games had argued the ad showed real gameplay, that 80%+ of players acquired through it were retained, and that a short ad cannot show the full game — [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/asa-rules-mobile-game-ad-was-misleading-gameplay-depicted-did-not-reflect-the-core-game-playing-experience)
- ASA vs Playrix (Homescapes/Gardenscapes, 2020): pin-pulling puzzle ads were ruled misleading because they were "not representative" of the games, even though they carried the disclaimer "Not all images represent actual gameplay". Playrix said the puzzles existed as mini-games, but admitted only about 0.03% of Homescapes users reached those levels. The ads were banned in that form — [TechRaptor](https://Techraptor.net/gaming/news/uk-asa-upholds-misleading-pin-pulling-puzzle-ad-claims-against-playrix); [Marketech APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/homescapes-gardenscapes-ads-banned-by-asa-for-misrepresentation/); [Frankfurt Kurnit advertising law blog](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102gij0/liar-liar-mobile-game-ads-under-fire-asa-bans-misleading-ads-for-mobile-games)
- TechRaptor notes that Hero Wars uses similar pin-puzzle ads while being mostly a casual clicker, but I found no ASA ruling on Hero Wars — [TechRaptor](https://Techraptor.net/gaming/news/uk-asa-upholds-misleading-pin-pulling-puzzle-ad-claims-against-playrix)
- Related 2025 ASA rulings:
  - Blackjackist (3 Sep 2025): a "No purchases" ad claim was misleading because the game had in-game purchases — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/asa-rules-that-kamagames-ltd-t-a-blackjackist-misled-consumers-with-no-purchases-advertising)
  - November 2025: three App Store listings were ruled misleading, including a spinner wheel whose equal segments implied equal odds — [Foot Anstey](https://www.footanstey.com/our-insights/articles-news/marketing-matters-review-of-november-2025/)
  - Kabam Games (19 Nov 2025): a complaint about loot boxes was upheld — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/blogs/law-now-blog/asa-upholds-complaint-against-kabam-games-over-loot-boxes)
- Google Play (2022 policy update): full-screen ads that cannot be closed after 15 seconds are not allowed, and ads may not interrupt the user at the start of a level. Coverage also says ads must not show content that does not accurately reflect the app, but I could not confirm the exact wording — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/technology/google-cracks-down-on-unexpected-misleading-ads-on-play-store-app-122072900281_1.html); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2022/07/29/google-play-ad-policy/)

### Inferences
- The genre's best-known "games" in ads (Last War, Evony, Top War-style 4X) are mostly not runner games at all. The runner is an acquisition hook. A parent who has seen those ads has seen something closer to our game than to Last War itself. In a sense, our game is the "real game from the fake ads", the same pitch that Last War's own campaign and "Real War (Not Fake)" used.
- The ASA tests whether the ad reflects the "core playing experience". If we ever advertise our game, the screenshots and video should show what a child actually plays most of the time.

### Gaps
- No ASA ruling specifically on Last War, Top War, Puzzles & Survival, Whiteout Survival or Royal Match was found. The ASA database at asa.org.uk was not searched directly.
- The exact content of Last War's in-game runner modes (Doomsday / Special Ops / Falcon Operation), their share of play and whether they exist in all versions could not be verified from official sources. Only aggregators and guides were available.
- Details of the refund-policy controversy (Korea, 2025) were not retrieved.
- No Deconstructor of Fun or GameRefinery teardown of gate-runner ads was found. Lancaric's article is paywalled.

## 2. The real runner games: core loops, gates, obstacles, finishes, level variety and meta-progression

### Takeaway
In the real hyper-casual and hybrid-casual titles, the core loop is short and the same everywhere: steer left/right, pick gates (+N, ×N, and red −N/÷N), avoid traps, clash with enemy crowds, then hit a finish. The finish is a staircase or bonus multiplier in Count Masters, a castle or King boss in Count Masters and Join Clash, and an enemy base in Mob Control. Variety comes from new obstacle types, moving gates and speed boosts, eras or themes, and different modes (cannon, bridge building, shooting). Meta-progression is a thin layer on top: coins buy upgrades to starting crowd size and gate bonuses, skins and weapons. Hybrid-casual hits (Mob Control) add card collections, base building, season passes and live events. Most of these meta systems are also monetization systems: ads, energy, passes and random packs.

### Inferences
- Gate types seen across sources: +N, ×N (×2/×3 most common), red/negative gates (−N, likely ÷N), moving gates, and gates you shoot to change (Mob Control, Timeline Up). Obstacles seen: axes, saws, danger zones, tires, negative zones, bricks, sliders, trampolines, zip wires and gaps needing bridges.
- Finish types: staircase/score tower (Count Masters), King or boss and castle capture (Count Masters, Join Clash), enemy base (Mob Control) and boss after waves (Real War). Our current boss with a health bar and rounds is in line with the genre.
- "Upgrades", "weapons" and "bridges" do exist in the real games, but mostly as separate titles or modes (Bridge Race, Trigger Up, Timeline Up) or as a meta layer (Count Masters' starting-crowd upgrade, Mob Control's cards). There is no single standard game that has everything.
- The commercial games are rated 13+ on the App Store and earn from ads, energy and passes. They are not designed for 6–14-year-olds and should not be copied wholesale.

- How often new mechanics are introduced per level (e.g., "a new obstacle every N levels") was not found in any source.
- Aquapark.io and "Gun Rush"/"Weapon Master" were not researched in detail.

## 3. What makes these games satisfying, and industry data on sessions, levels and retention

### Takeaway
The appeal is "number goes up": seeing the crowd multiply ("strangely satisfying"), simple decisions under time pressure, and lots of audio-visual feedback. Research on "juice" shows that moderate feedback beats both none and excessive, and that feedback tied to the player's success motivates better than blanket amplification. Industry data: hypercasual sessions are short (about 2.5–3.5 min). Count Masters averaged 212 s per session in 2022 with 42% D1 retention. Shortening levels from 2 min to 1 min improved metrics in a Supersonic case study.

### Cited Findings
- Sensor Tower hybrid-casual playbook (2023): Count Masters (hypercasual) averaged 212 s per session in 2022, vs 372 s for Stumble Guys (hybrid-casual). 68% of Count Masters sessions exceeded 1 minute and 23% exceeded 10 minutes. D1 retention was 42% for Count Masters vs 54% for Stumble Guys. Hybrid-casual downloads reached 5B in 2022 while hypercasual downloads fell 15% — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/81227/hybridcasual-games-hit-5-billion-downloads-in-2022/); [Game Industry Library (Sensor Tower playbook)](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/hybrid-casual-games-playbook-2023/read). Caveat: later playbook editions mislabel the retention figures, and these are single-title comparisons.
- Supersonic (Aug 2021): at least 4 of the top 20 hypercasual games (Apr–Jun) were runners, and runners stayed in the charts longer than puzzle or simulation games. The average hypercasual session is 2 min 39 s. Supersonic's case study: cutting Hide 'N Seek levels from 2 min to 1 min (plus other tweaks) raised playtime 33% and D1 retention to 48%. Bright colours worked better: in Stacky Dash, a bright pink layout instead of pale green cut Facebook CPI from $0.40 to $0.25. The article advises avoiding too many colours and playtesting with outsiders to lower difficulty — [Supersonic blog](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/runner-games-are-here-to-stay-how-can-you-capitalize-on-the-trend/)
- Hypercasual design guides recommend getting the player into the first playable action within about 5 seconds, using many short loops rather than few long ones, and making sure a player feels they achieved something in a few seconds (a "Starbucks test") — [Moloco](https://www.moloco.com/blog/how-to-make-hyper-casual-games); [Mobile Free to Play](https://mobilefreetoplay.com/how-to-make-hyper-casual-games/) (vendor/publisher blogs; I did not map each tip to a specific page)
- Mob Control's store copy itself sells the "strangely satisfying" feeling of watching the mob multiply — [Nintendo eShop](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/mob-control-switch/)
- "Juice it or lose it" (Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho, 2012) showed a plain Breakout clone becoming fun through flash, screen shake, floating text, sound and particles. Sources disagree on the venue (GDC vs Nordic Game Jam). Jan Willem Nijman (Vlambeer) gave a separate talk on screenshake. Folmer Kelly (GDC Europe) warned against "juice for the sake of juice", which can distract from the main goal — [RPG Playground](https://rpgplayground.com/research-making-a-juicy-game/); [Easton Dev blog](https://eastondev.com/blog/en/posts/dev/20260521-game-feedback-feel/); [itch.io "Juicy Break"](https://crcdng.itch.io/juicy-break)
- Kao (2020, Entertainment Computing): four versions of an action RPG differed only in feedback. Medium and High juiciness beat both No and Extreme juiciness on all measures; juice "has to be applied adequately to the situation" — [Improbable Research / Goodreads summary](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/19932694-the-effects-of-juiciness-in-an-action-rpg-new-study); [Designing Game Feel survey, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09201)
- CHI 2024, a pre-registered experiment with N=1,699: success-dependent feedback raised all motives (effectance, competence, curiosity), while pure amplification lowered them, possibly by undermining agency. Curiosity was the strongest predictor of enjoyment and the only predictor of free-choice playtime — [Imperial College Spiral](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/253c32f5-d124-4a23-88b5-9aaaf114608d)

### Inferences
- For a math game: make the celebration scale with what the child did. For example, a bigger burst for a correct answer to a harder problem, crowd growth animated soldier by soldier, a short slow-motion moment on a correct gate, a gentle shake on a crowd clash. Do not add juice everywhere. Uncertainty ("will my 37 soldiers beat their 40?") feeds curiosity, the strongest playtime predictor.
- Level length of about 45–90 s per run segment, with the first input within seconds, matches genre norms. The children's version may need a little more reading time per problem than a pure arcade game.
- Bright, limited palettes are cheap, effective variety.

### Gaps
- No GDC talk or Deconstructor of Fun analysis specific to gate runners was found. No Liftoff or GameRefinery runner benchmark was retrieved.
- No source gave level length in seconds for Count Masters or Mob Control specifically.

## 4. Existing educational math games in arcade formats: mechanics, integration and criticism

### Takeaway
Most math games bolt questions onto gameplay (answer correctly → get a move or reward). Prodigy is the best-known example and has been criticised for its membership upsell. Research on "intrinsic integration" (Zombie Division, 2011; Pac-Man-like replication, 2022) shows children learn more, and play up to ~7× longer by choice, when the math is the game mechanic itself rather than a gate in front of it. A gate runner where the gate values are the math (e.g., "+12 or ×3?") is a natural fit for intrinsic integration. DragonBox shows a risk: skills learned inside a disguised game may not transfer to standard notation.

### Cited Findings
- Zombie Division (Habgood & Ainsworth, Journal of the Learning Sciences, 2011; ages 7–11): Study 1 had 58 children playing intrinsic, extrinsic or control versions for 2 hours; Study 2 had 16 children choosing freely. The intrinsic version (math built into the core mechanic) produced more learning in fixed time, and children spent about 7× longer playing it in free time — [Sheffield Hallam repository](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/)
- Cutting & Iacovides (2022, pre-registered, n=210, a Pac-Man-like game): the intrinsically integrated version again gave higher learning, with motivation and cognitive load unchanged. The authors attribute the benefit to attention rather than motivation — [White Rose eprints](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/189921/1/LearningByDoingCuttingAndIacovides090822.pdf)
- Prodigy Math Game: the core loop is turn-based battles where answering math questions gives magic and casts spells. Players catch and train monsters; correct answer = move, wrong answer = no move — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodigy_Math_Game); [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/prodigy-math-game)
- Common Sense Media parent reviews criticise Prodigy's membership model and educational effectiveness, with many calling it a money-making scheme (anecdotal) — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/prodigy-math-game)
- FTC complaint (19 Feb 2021): 22 groups led by the Campaign for a Commercial-Free Childhood (now Fairplay) said Prodigy's free school version pushes kids to play at home, where ads and persuasive design "relentlessly" promote membership ($59.88–$107.40/yr). The complaint says children "see up to four times more advertisements than math exercises", that membership perks visible in class create "two classes of students", and that learning claims lack substantiation. Prodigy disputed the allegations — [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/popular-interactive-math-game-prodigy-is-target-of-complaint-to-federal-trade-commission/2021/02); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/prodigy-math-game-ftc-complaint-d693af67-019a-4ff5-9bff-8c73d1277342.html); [complaint PDF](https://fairplayforkids.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Prodigy_Complaint_Feb21.pdf)
- Fairplay's 2021 annual report said the FTC was investigating. I found no later outcome — [Fairplay 2021 report](https://fairplayforkids.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/2021-Annual-report.pdf)
- Times Tables Rock Stars (UK): timed and live competitive modes, coins and avatar (rock-star) customisation. One school guide describes "Gig" mode as 5 minutes, up to 100 questions, 10 coins per correct answer — [Abel Smith School](https://www.abelsmith.herts.sch.uk/tt-rock-stars/); [Collingham School](https://www.collinghamschool.com/ttrs-sumdog) (school guides; I did not confirm which page holds which figure)
- A 2024 academic critique (Thomson & Wright, DECP Debate) says no formal effectiveness studies of TTRS exist — [Southampton Ed Psych blog](https://blog.soton.ac.uk/edpsych/?p=1410)
- Provider-side data cited by Shine Trust: for 900k+ children, average answer time fell from 9 s to 4 s per question (not independent) — [Shine Trust](https://shinetrust.org.uk/?p=1178)
- DragonBox evidence is mixed:
  - A Carnegie Mellon study (reported by EdSurge, 2016) found it "ineffective" on standard equation-solving tests; learning in the game "does not transfer out of the game" when the concrete context hides the equations — [EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2016-03-13-enter-the-dragonbox-can-a-game-really-teach-third-graders-algebra)
  - A 2023 RCT (n=887, grade 7) found sustained conceptual gains but procedural gains that faded by the posttest — [EdUHK repository](https://repository.eduhk.hk/en/publications/examining-shifts-in-conceptual-knowledge-procedural-knowledge-and/)
  - A small quasi-experiment (2016, n=60) found positive effects — [eJMT](https://ejmt.mathandtech.org/Abstracts/eJMT_v10n1p1A.html)
- Math Run: Panda Chase (iOS): a runner where you avoid obstacles while solving problems, aligned to the US Common Core for K–5, with rewards for correct answers — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id785296395)

### Inferences
- Our current design (problem shown, then two gates with candidate answers) is "extrinsic": the answer choice is a quiz. A stronger intrinsic variant is gates that show operations rather than answers (e.g., "+8" vs "×2" with 6 soldiers now), where choosing well requires calculating. Grade-specific forms: fractions ("½ of crowd" vs "+15"), percentages ("+20%" vs "+30"), negatives ("−5" vs "×(−1)" in grade 7–8 is probably too abstract), powers ("²").
- Show standard notation on the gates. This avoids the DragonBox transfer problem, because the child sees normal school notation.
- Prodigy's lessons: no membership, no visible paid cosmetics, and no ads replacing math time.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of Prodigy's learning effect was found.
- Reception of "Monster Math" and Math Playground titles was not researched.
- No research on gate-runner math games specifically was found.

## 5. Environment variety, adaptable mechanics, and what to avoid for children

### Takeaway
I found no reliable genre-wide list of environments. The real variety comes from different obstacle sets and modes (Bridge Race for bridges, Mob Control for cannons, Timeline Up for eras with weapon evolution, Join Clash for axes and saws), plus bright colour themes. Many meta systems in commercial runners are manipulative and regulated: energy, passes, random booster packs and loot boxes, spinner wheels, forced or frequent ads, "come back tomorrow" lures and time-limited offers. A children's game should keep earn-only progression (upgrades unlocked by play and correct answers), deterministic rewards, and no purchases or ads.

### Cited Findings
- Timeline Up! progresses through historical eras "each with unique visuals and challenges" (named eras not listed) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/6475592923)
- Bright colours outperform darker ones in runner gameplay and creatives; avoid too many colours — [Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/runner-games-are-here-to-stay-how-can-you-capitalize-on-the-trend/)
- Radesky et al. (JAMA Network Open, 2022, 160 apps used by 3–5-year-olds): only 20% of apps had no manipulative design. Patterns included parasocial pressure, invented time limits, navigation roadblocks and lures such as "Come back tomorrow and get a dragon". Children from lower-SES households were more exposed, and nearly 99% of children had at least one such app among their most used — [Deceptive Design](https://deceptive.design/articles/prevalence-and-characteristics-of-manipulative-design-in-mobile-applications-used-by-children); [Michigan Medicine](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/design-tricks-commonly-used-monetize-young-childrens-app-use)
- EU CPC Network "Key principles on in-game virtual currencies" (March 2025, non-binding "minimum requirement"): clear real-money prices, no hiding the cost of content, no forcing unwanted currency purchases, and game design that respects vulnerabilities, especially children's. The parallel action against Star Stable flagged direct appeals to children to buy and time-limited sales pressure — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/03/26/eu-bodies-act-against-gaming-companies-to-protect-children-from-in-game-purchase-102k658); [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/cpcn-announces-virtual-currency-consumer-protection-guidelines/)
- UK ICO Age Appropriate Design Code, Standard 13 "Nudge techniques": do not use design features that nudge children to give up unnecessary data, weaken privacy or extend their use — [ICO Code PDF](https://ico.org.uk/media/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services-2-1.pdf)
- Apple Kids Category (Guideline 1.3, 2019): third-party ads and analytics restricted in kids' apps, later softened from "may not" to "should not" with limited exceptions. Current wording not verified — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/12/apple-tweaks-app-store-rule-changes-for-childrens-apps-and-sign-in-services/); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2019/06/03/apple-kids-privacy/)
- Commercial examples of monetized meta systems:
  - Timeline Up: energy packs, a Timeline Pass, diamonds, a starter pack — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/6475592923)
  - Mob Control: random-rarity booster packs, a monthly Season Pass, interstitial ads — [Nintendo](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/mob-control-switch/); [Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/mob-control)
  - Count Masters: frequent ads, which reviewers see as a churn problem; "Simulated Gambling" listed on its age rating — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/count-masters-crowd-runner-3d/id1568245971); [Marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/intel-report/games/count-masters-crowd-runner-3d)

### Inferences
**Mechanics to adapt (earn-only, no money)**
1. **Operation gates instead of answer gates** (intrinsic math): +N, −N, ×N, ÷N and, for higher levels, fractions, percentages, squares. The child picks the better gate by calculating. Occasionally show "which gate is better?" as the problem itself.
2. **Staircase or multiplier finish** (Count Masters): soldiers climb steps marked ×1.1…×2 or 10, 20, 30. Use it for a quick estimation question ("how many steps will 47 soldiers reach if each step takes 5?").
3. **Crowd vs crowd clash as subtraction**: show "48 vs 30 → ?" before the clash and let the child predict the result. The animation confirms it.
4. **Cannon level** (Mob Control) as a different mode: aim the cannon at the gate with the right answer, or fire through ×2 gates toward an enemy base; the boss base has a health bar.
5. **Bridge building** (Bridge Race): a gap in the bridge needs exactly N blocks or planks; collect blocks by solving problems. Too few and you cannot cross. This teaches exact counting, measurement and fractions ("¾ of the bridge is built, how many planks are missing?").
6. **Weapon or era evolution** (Timeline Up, Trigger Up): each level (school grade) is an era or world. Soldiers' equipment visually improves (sticks → swords → bows → cannons → lasers) as a reward for answer streaks or level completion. Never purchasable.
7. **New obstacles per world**: axes and saws (Join Clash), moving gates and speed boosts (Mob Control), trampolines, zip wires, ladders, lifts and sliders (Bridge Race).
8. **Starting-crowd upgrade bought with coins earned in play** (Count Masters): acceptable if coins come only from play. Cap upgrades so math choices still matter.
9. **Short runs** (about 1 minute per segment), quick start, and moderate success-dependent juice.

**Environment ideas** (my inference; no source gives a genre catalogue)
- Bridge: gaps, swinging planks.
- Canyon: narrow paths, falling rocks.
- City: cars, rooftops.
- Desert: sandstorm reduces visibility, quicksand.
- Snow: ice slows or slides, snowballs.
- Lava: jump pads over lava, a fire gate.
- Sea or ships: waves move gates.
- Space: low gravity, asteroid fields.
- Map one environment per level (grade) or per topic. Change the colour palette per world.

**To avoid with children** (manipulative or regulated)
- Energy or lives timers.
- Season or battle passes.
- Random booster packs, loot boxes, spinner wheels, "mystery chests" with hidden odds.
- Interstitial or rewarded ads.
- "No ads" purchases.
- Time-limited offers and countdowns.
- Daily-login streak pressure and "come back tomorrow" lures, parasocial characters guilting the child.
- Visible paid cosmetics creating classroom status differences (Prodigy).
- Misleading store screenshots.
- Realistic guns or gore (the commercial titles are rated 13+).
- Leaderboards against strangers.

### Gaps
- No reliable source listing environments and themes across Count Masters, Mob Control and similar titles was found.
- No evidence was found on how often top runners add new mechanics (e.g., a new obstacle every N levels).
- No research directly testing weapon or era evolution as motivation in educational games was found.
