# Publishing a free children's math game (ages ~6–14) on Google Play and the App Store as an individual in Poland: store rules and legal obligations (state as of 20 October 2026)

Context for the report writer: the game ("Śmierdzipacha") is a web/HTML5 game (`gra.js`) that would be wrapped as a mobile app. Its newer versions include zombies, things to shoot, weapons, gold and an armory ("Zbrojownia"). That affects age ratings and the HTML-wrapper rules below. Research date: 20 Oct 2026. Most sources were read directly; a few came only from search-result summaries and are marked as such.

## 1. Google Play: account, fee, identity verification, mandatory closed test, Android developer verification, technical minimums

### Takeaway
A personal (individual) Play Console account costs a one-time US$25 and needs a government ID, card and Android-device verification. Personal accounts created after 13 Nov 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days before they can apply for production. The 2026 "Android developer verification" sideloading rules barely affect a Play-only developer: Play registers apps automatically, and enforcement in 2026 covers only Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand. Since 31 Aug 2026, new apps and updates must target API level 36 (Android 16).

### Cited Findings
**Account and fee**
- "There is a US$25 one-time registration fee." Accepted cards are MasterCard, Visa, Amex, Discover (US only) and Visa Electron (outside US only). Prepaid cards are not accepted. You "must be at least 18 years of age to sign up". You "may be asked for a valid government ID and a credit card, both under your legal name". If the information is judged invalid, the fee is not refunded. — [Play Console Help: Get started with Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en)
- Since early 2024, new personal accounts must verify access to an Android device using the Play Console mobile app. — [Play Console Help: Get started](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en)
- There are two account types, Personal and Organization. For a **personal account**, Google shows your legal name, country and developer email on Google Play, and **if you monetize, also your full address**. Personal accounts must provide and verify a developer email. Organization accounts need a **D-U-N-S number**, and the legal name and address in the payments profile must match Dun & Bradstreet. Organizations must show a verified phone and email publicly. D-U-N-S registration is free. Account type cannot be changed on an existing payments profile. (From the search-result summary of these Help pages, not read in full.) — [Required information to create a Play Console developer account](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312?hl=en); [Verify your developer identity information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10841920?hl=en); [Contact information requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10840893?hl=en)

**Mandatory closed test (personal accounts)**
- Google Play "requires personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023, to test their apps" before distribution. You need a closed test with **at least 12 testers** opted in at the time you apply, who "must have been opted in continuously for the preceding 14 days". The days must be consecutive. A tester who opts out and back in starts the count over. You apply via Dashboard → "Apply for production", a form with three sections: "About your closed test", "About your app/game" and "About your production readiness". Review is "usually seven days or less", and Google may require more testing. — [Play Console Help: App testing requirements for new personal developer accounts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)
- History, low reliability: third-party guides say the original requirement was 20 testers, later reduced to 12. One developer-forum report dates the Play Console wording change to about 11 Dec 2024, with no official announcement. Organization accounts are exempt from this test. The official page lists only the current 12/14 rule. — [testerscommunity.com guide](https://www.testerscommunity.com/blog/google-play-closed-testing-requirements-2026); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-play-app-testing-requirement-3510580)

**Android developer verification (sideloading changes)**
- Official timeline: **August 2026**, developer APIs, "limited distribution accounts" and the power-user "advanced flow" launch. **30 Sept 2026**, protections start for users installing from participating stores in **Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand** (certified devices, Android 7+). **2027 and beyond**, global expansion to all apps on certified devices. "Google Play automatically registers 99% of apps". Play Console can manually register the rest or apps distributed outside Play. Limited distribution accounts let students, teachers and hobbyists share apps with **up to 20 devices without a government-issued ID or registration fee**. — [developer.android.com/developer-verification](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification)
- Press reports: unregistered apps can still be installed via ADB or the "advanced flow", which has warnings and a one-day wait. Dates have shifted before: an earlier timeline gave a March 2026 global start. — [Android Authority timeline](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-sideloading-changes-timeline-3679204/); [Help Net Security, 31 Mar 2026](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/31/android-developer-verification-requirement/)

**Technical minimum (target API)**
- From **31 Aug 2026**, new apps and app updates must target **Android 16 (API 36)**. Wear OS/Automotive target API 35, TV/XR target API 34. Existing apps must target API 35+ to remain available to new users on newer devices. An extension to **1 Nov 2026** can be requested. — [Android Developers: Target API level requirements](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)

### Inferences
- Checklist (Google): (1) create a **personal** account as an adult with Polish ID and card; (2) verify the Android device in the Play Console app; (3) recruit **≥12 real testers** (family, classmates' parents) and keep them opted in for **14 consecutive days**; (4) apply for production; (5) build the wrapper to target **API 36**.
- If the game shows ads (monetizes), a personal account will show the developer's **full home address publicly**. This is a privacy cost for a private person, and it is similar to the DSA "trader" disclosure (section 6).
- Android developer verification does not change anything for a Play-only release from Poland this winter. It matters only if the family shares an APK outside Play once global enforcement starts in 2027. A free limited-distribution account (up to 20 devices) would cover that.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full text of the identity-verification pages, so exact documents accepted for Polish individuals (ID card or passport, proof of address) were not confirmed.
- No official Google source states when the tester count fell from 20 to 12.
- The Android developer verification fee for Android Developer Console (non-Play) accounts is not on the overview page.

## 2. Google Play Families Policy: target audience, ads, data, Teacher Approved, mixed audience

### Takeaway
An app whose target audience includes children (6–14 does) falls under the Families Policy. Rules: an accurate "Target audience and content" declaration; no transmission of the Advertising ID or other device identifiers from children; ads to children or unknown-age users only through **Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs** (e.g., AdMob ≥ 19.0.0), with **no interest-based ads or remarketing** and strict format limits; no plain webview-of-a-website apps; a privacy policy. Because 6–14 includes teens, it is a "mixed audience" case. A neutral age screen is needed only if you want to treat older users differently. Otherwise treat everyone as a child.

### Cited Findings
**Target audience and content**
- You must select target age groups in Play Console's **Target Audience and Content** section before publishing. Select multiple groups only if the app is designed for and appropriate for all of them. Apps for babies, toddlers and preschoolers should select only "Ages 5 & Under". Child-oriented imagery and wording can affect Google's own assessment. Changing the declaration needs an app update to show in the store. Misrepresentation "may result in removal or suspension of your app". — [Google Play Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)

**Families Policy requirements (apps targeting children)**
- **App functionality:** apps "may not simply be a webview of a website", or have the main purpose of driving affiliate traffic without the website owner's permission. — [Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- **Play Console answers:** Target Audience and Content, **Data safety** and the **IARC content rating questionnaire** must be accurate and kept current. — [Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- **Data practices:** apps solely targeting children must not transmit AAID, SIM Serial, Build Serial, BSSID, MAC, SSID, IMEI or IMSI. "Apps solely targeted to children should not request AD_ID permission when targeting Android API 33 or higher." Mixed-audience apps must not transmit these identifiers from children **or users of unknown age**. No phone number from TelephonyManager. Apps solely targeting children may not request location permission or collect precise location. — [Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- **APIs/SDKs:** child-only apps may not include SDKs not approved for child-directed services. Mixed-audience apps may use them only behind a **neutral age screen**, or in a way that collects no child data. — [Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- **Social features** need an online-safety reminder and "adult action" (PIN, password, birthdate and so on) before children exchange personal information. **Legal compliance:** apps and SDKs must comply with "COPPA, GDPR, and other applicable laws". — [Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)

**Ads rules for children and unknown-age users**
- Use only **Google Play Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs**, with no interest-based advertising or remarketing, child-appropriate ad content only, Families ad-format rules, and compliance with ad laws and industry standards for children. — [Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- **Prohibited formats** include: ads that fill the screen or interfere with use without a clear dismiss option, including ad walls; **rewarded/opt-in ads that cannot be closed after 5 seconds**; **interstitials immediately on app launch**; multiple ad placements on one page; ads not clearly distinguishable from content, including offerwalls; shocking or emotionally manipulative tactics; deceptive dismiss buttons; **failing to distinguish virtual game currency from real-money purchases**. Ads "must not have deceptive content or be designed in a way that will result in inadvertent clicks". Banned ad content includes alcohol, gambling (including free sweepstakes), dating, violent content, inappropriate games and more. — [Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- **Self-certified SDK list** (no date shown, ©2026). It includes **Google AdMob (com.google.android.gms:play-services-ads) minimum 19.0.0**, plus Unity Ads 4.0.1, ironSource 7.2.1, Kidoz 8.9.4, SuperAwesome 8.4.3, Chartboost 9.1.1, InMobi 10.5.5 and others. The program "is currently not accepting new applicants". AppLovin left the program. Child-only apps must use only self-certified versions. Mixed-audience apps may use non-certified SDKs only for non-child users (e.g., after a neutral age screen). With real-time bidding you must review and rate all creatives or partner only with self-certified sources. — [Play Console Help: Families Self-Certified Ads SDK list](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12955712?hl=en); [Participate in the Families Ads Program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9283445)
- AdMob: Android apps classified as Families must use a Families self-certified ads SDK or mediation when serving ads to children or users of unknown age. AdMob blocks non-certified ad sources in apps designed for children. (Search-result summary.) — [AdMob Help](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6223431?hl=en)

**Mixed audience and Teacher Approved**
- Mixed-audience apps must include a **neutral age screen** so that children get only non-personalized ads from certified SDKs, and must have an accurate privacy policy. Apps that comply with the Families policies can opt in to be rated for **Teacher Approved**. Inclusion is not guaranteed. — [Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)

**Privacy policy and Data safety (all apps)**
- The privacy-policy link must go in Play Console (Policy → App content → Privacy policy) **and** within the app. Per Play Console Help, even apps that do not access any personal or sensitive data must submit a privacy policy. Apps aimed at children need one regardless of permissions. The URL must be active, public and not a PDF. All developers must complete the **Data safety** section. (Search-result summary of the User Data policy pages.) — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en); [Data safety section](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en)

### Inferences
- For a wrapped HTML5 game, **bundle the game files inside the app**. A thin wrapper (TWA/webview) that loads the live website risks breaking the "not simply a webview of a website" rule.
- Simplest compliant ad setup for 6–14: treat **all users as children**. Use AdMob ≥ 19 configured for child-directed, non-personalized ads, do not declare the AD_ID permission, put no interstitial at launch, make any rewarded ad closable within 5 s, and use only one ad placement per screen. A neutral age screen adds complexity with little benefit for this audience.
- If the in-game "gold" can never be bought with real money, say so clearly in the game and store listing to stay clear of the "virtual currency vs real money" rule.
- The cleanest option for kids, especially for Teacher Approved chances, is no ads at all.

### Gaps
- The exact Play Console age-group labels beyond "Ages 5 & Under" were not re-verified in this pass.
- Teacher Approved selection criteria were not read in detail (separate Help page).
- Google's EU ad-consent requirement (Google-certified CMP for EEA ad traffic) was not verified in this pass.

## 3. Apple App Store: Developer Program, Mac/Xcode, Kids Category (1.3, 5.1.4), age ratings 2025+, Declared Age Range, 4.2/4.7

### Takeaway
The Apple Developer Program costs 99 USD per year, shown in local currency at enrollment; the PLN amount was not found. An individual enrolls with an Apple Account with 2FA, and their **legal name appears as seller**. Builds must be made with the iOS 26 SDK (since 28 Apr 2026) and uploaded with Mac-only tools. Kids Category apps (bands 5 & under, 6–8, 9–11) must not include third-party analytics or third-party ads, and need **parental gates** before links out and purchases. The new age ratings are 4+, 9+, 13+, 16+ and 18+. Cartoon or fantasy violence, weapons or horror push a game to **9+**, or 13+ if frequent. Under 4.2, a "repackaged website" may be rejected.

### Cited Findings
**Program, enrollment, cost**
- "99 USD per membership year". Prices vary by region and are shown in local currency during enrollment. Fee waivers exist only for eligible nonprofits, accredited educational institutions and government entities. — [Apple Developer Program: Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- Individuals need an Apple Account with **two-factor authentication**, must be of legal age of majority (otherwise a parent enrolls), and must use their **legal name** (aliases or company names delay approval). Apple verifies legal name, email, phone and address (no P.O. boxes). "Your legal name is displayed as the **seller name** on the App Store." Organizations must be a legal entity (DBAs and trade names are not accepted), need a **D-U-N-S Number**, a domain email and a public website. — [Apple Developer Program: Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)

**Mac / Xcode / SDK**
- Builds are uploaded with **Xcode, Swift Playground, altool (xcrun) or Transporter**, a macOS app from the Mac App Store. (Search-result summary.) — [App Store Connect Help: Upload builds](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-builds/upload-builds/)
- From **28 Apr 2026**, iOS/iPadOS apps must be built with the **iOS 26 SDK or later** (news of 3 Feb 2026). From **April 2027**, the **iOS 27 SDK** is required (news of 9 Sep 2026). — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/)

**Kids Category and kids' data**
- **Guideline 1.3 Kids Category:** apps "must not include links out of the app, purchasing opportunities, or other distractions to kids unless reserved for a designated area behind a parental gate … Kids Category apps may not send personally identifiable information or device information to third parties. Apps in the Kids Category should not include third-party analytics or third-party advertising." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Guideline 5.1.4 Kids:** (a) review COPPA, GDPR and other laws. Apps may ask for birthdate and parental contact only to comply with those laws. "Apps intended primarily for kids should not include third-party analytics or third-party advertising." (b) "In limited cases, third-party analytics and third-party advertising may be permitted provided that the services adhere to the same terms set forth in Guideline 1.3." Kids Category apps and apps that collect or share minors' personal information "must include a privacy policy". — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Guideline 2.3.8:** all metadata (icons, screenshots, previews) must fit a 4+ rating. "Use of terms like 'For Kids' and 'For Children' in app metadata is reserved in the App Store for the Kids Category." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Guideline 5.1.1(i):** "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app". The policy must cover what is collected, third parties and retention/deletion. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple's kids page: choose Kids Category band "**ages 5 and under, 6-8, or 9-11**". Parental gates are "adult-level tasks" that stop kids from buying In-App Purchases or following links out. A voice-over prompt is suggested for pre-readers. "Kids apps should not transmit personally identifiable information or device information to third parties — even in sections intended for adults — unless the parent explicitly consents." "If your app or game includes advertising, … this content must be human-reviewed for age appropriateness in order to be displayed." — [Apple: Design safe and age-appropriate experiences](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/kids-apps/)

**Minimum functionality / HTML5**
- **4.2:** "Your app should include features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website. If your app is not particularly useful, unique, or 'app-like,' it doesn't belong on the App Store." **4.2.2:** apps shouldn't primarily be "web clippings, content aggregators, or a collection of links". — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **4.7** covers HTML5/JavaScript mini apps and mini games "not embedded in the binary". You are responsible for them, and 4.7.x adds privacy, filtering and age-restriction duties. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Guidelines were updated on 13 Nov 2025 (including 4.7), 8 Jun 2026 and **16 Oct 2026** (1.2 NCII, 2.5.19 bait-and-switch/hidden features, new 2.3.14 creative assets). — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/)

**Age ratings (since 2025) and Declared Age Range**
- The ratings are **4+, 9+, 13+, 16+, 18+**, plus "Unrated", which "can't be published on the App Store". 4+ may include parental controls, age assurance, UGC, messaging, **advertising** and infrequent contests. **9+** covers "infrequent cartoon or fantasy violence", "infrequent guns or other weapons", infrequent horror/fear themes, infrequent profanity or crude humor, and **loot boxes**. **13+** covers "frequent cartoon or fantasy violence", frequent horror and social media. The questionnaire has these categories: In-App Controls, Capabilities, Mature Themes, Medical/Wellness, Sexuality/Nudity, Violence, Chance-Based Activities. "Age ratings for an app may vary based on the OS version." Australia, Brazil and Korea have regional ratings. — [App Store Connect Help: Age ratings](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/age-ratings)
- Apple moved to five tiers and dropped 12+ and 17+ (announced July 2025). The questionnaire was expanded and developers can raise their rating. — [TechCrunch, 25 Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/25/apple-broadens-app-stores-age-rating-system)
- 9 Jul 2026: the age-rating questionnaire adds social-media questions, and "Beginning in September 2026, responses will be required when submitting new apps or updates". — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/)
- **Declared Age Range API:** parents can let children share their Child Account's age range ("Always, Ask First, or Never"). The birthdate isn't provided. Where law requires, it also returns an age category, the assurance method, and whether consent is required for a "significant change". Related tools: **PermissionKit Significant Change API**, StoreKit `ageRatingCode`, App Store Server Notifications on withdrawn consent, and Ask to Buy. — [Apple kids page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/kids-apps/); [WWDC25 session 299](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/299/)
- The API is available worldwide on iOS/iPadOS/macOS 26+ (23 Dec 2025). On 24 Feb 2026 Apple published "Age requirements for apps distributed in Brazil, Australia, Singapore, Utah, and Louisiana" (API updates in beta). — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/)

### Inferences
- **Mac:** every official upload path (Xcode, Transporter, altool) runs on macOS, so in practice you need a Mac, a rented cloud Mac or a cloud CI that builds on macOS. This is an inference; I did not verify cloud services.
- **Kids Category vs ads:** in the Kids Category, third-party ads are practically excluded. Only "limited cases" are allowed, with human-reviewed creatives. Recommendation: on iOS, either **no ads + Kids Category** (6–8 or 9–11 band) or skip the Kids Category. Even outside it, an app "intended primarily for kids" is under 5.1.4.
- **Age rating risk for this game:** zombies, shooting and weapons in a cartoon style likely make it **9+** ("infrequent cartoon/fantasy violence", "guns or other weapons", "horror/fear themes"), or **13+** if violence is "frequent". The name and gags may also count as "crude humor". A 13+ rating would clash with a 6–14 audience and the Kids Category. Answer the questionnaire honestly (Guideline 2.3.6) and consider softening the content.
- **4.2 risk:** bundle all assets offline and add app-like features (offline play, local save, sound settings, parental gate, no browser UI). A wrapper that loads the website is the classic 4.2 rejection. 4.7 applies only if games are downloaded at runtime rather than embedded.

### Gaps
- PLN price of the Apple Developer Program for Poland: not found in official sources (Apple shows the local price only during enrollment).
- Whether Apple formally requires an age-rating band for Kids Category apps (e.g., must be 4+ or 9+) was not found explicitly.
- The earlier deadline (reported as 31 Jan 2026) to answer the new age-rating questions was not confirmed on Apple's pages read.

## 4. Privacy law: GDPR/RODO for children in Poland, privacy-policy requirement for an app with no data collection

### Takeaway
GDPR Art. 8 sets 16 as the default age for a child's own consent to "information society services", and member states may lower it to no less than 13. **Poland did not lower it, so 16 applies.** A draft lowering it to 13 was dropped in 2018. Art. 8 matters only when processing relies on consent. A game with no accounts, no analytics and a local-only save processes almost no personal data. Both stores still **require a privacy-policy URL** for every app.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR Art. 8: processing a child's data on the basis of consent for information society services is lawful where the child is at least 16. Member states may set a lower age, "not below 13". — [GDPR Art. 8 (gdpr.pl, Polish text)](https://gdpr.pl/baza-wiedzy/akty-prawne/interaktywny-tekst-gdpr/artykul-8-warunki-wyrazenia-zgody-przez-dziecko-w-przypadku-uslug-spoleczenstwa-informacyjnego); [GDPR on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- Poland: the Ministry of Digital Affairs' draft proposed 13 (to match the Civil Code), but the provision was removed from the government draft adopted at the end of March 2018. With no national rule, GDPR's 16 applies. The Polish data protection act does not regulate the age threshold. — [Rzeczpospolita: "RODO: Facebook i e-mail dozwolone od lat szesnastu"](https://www.rp.pl/dane-osobowe/art1957201-rodo-facebook-i-e-mail-dozwolone-od-lat-szesnastu); [GazetaPrawna](https://prawo.gazetaprawna.pl/artykuly/1113981,rodo-dane-osobowe-dzieci-pod-ochrona-az-do-16-lat.html); [GDPR Risk Tracker](https://www.gdprrisktracker.pl/produkt/aktualnosci/social-media-tylko-dla-16/)
- A separate Polish petition for a 16+ minimum age on social platforms reached the Senate, but it "nie stanowi jeszcze projektu ustawy" (is not yet a bill). — [GDPR Risk Tracker](https://www.gdprrisktracker.pl/produkt/aktualnosci/social-media-tylko-dla-16/)
- UODO has published material on children's digital safety and responsibility (not read in full). — [UODO: Bezpieczeństwo cyfrowe dzieci i młodzieży a odpowiedzialność](https://www.uodo.gov.pl/pl/file/3502)
- Privacy policy is mandatory on both stores. Apple 5.1.1(i): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app". Google: even apps that don't access personal data must submit a privacy policy, and it must be both in the Play Console field and in the app. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Play User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en)

### Inferences
- With **no ads and no analytics**, a short, honest privacy policy in Polish and English is enough: "we collect nothing; progress is saved only on the device; no third parties". Host it on a public URL (not a PDF), link it in both consoles and inside the app, and keep Data safety and Apple App Privacy answers consistent ("no data collected").
- **Adding ads changes this.** The ad SDK processes device data and IP addresses. You would need a lawful basis, and for EU users likely consent under ePrivacy rules, which for under-16s in Poland would have to come from a parent. Non-personalized, child-directed ad settings reduce but do not remove this. A lawyer or UODO guidance is advisable before turning ads on.

### Gaps
- No 2026 UODO guidance specific to children's mobile games was found.
- The Polish ePrivacy implementation (Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej) and consent for non-personalized ads were not researched.
- I found no confirmed 2025–2026 change to the Polish age of digital consent. One search summary suggested a recent removal of the 13-year clause, but the dated sources point to 2018.

## 5. US law if US children can download: COPPA (amended Rule 2025/2026) and state App Store Accountability Acts (Texas, Utah, Louisiana, Alabama, California AB 1043)

### Takeaway
COPPA applies to **foreign** operators of services directed to US children under 13. The amended COPPA Rule took effect 23 Jun 2025, with full compliance due **22 Apr 2026**. It requires **separate parental consent for disclosing children's data to third parties such as ad networks**, plus a written retention policy and a written security program. Of the state app-store laws, **only Texas is in force** (Apple applies it to new Texas accounts from 4 Jun 2026). Utah moved to 6 May 2027, Louisiana to 1 Jul 2027, Alabama and California AB 1043 start 1 Jan 2027. For a developer of a free game without accounts, the main duties are an accurate age rating, using the store's age and consent signals if relevant, and flagging "significant changes".

### Cited Findings
**COPPA**
- COPPA covers foreign-based websites and online services that are directed to children in the US or that knowingly collect personal information from US children. — [FTC: Complying with COPPA, FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions); [FTC blog 2018 (warning letters; Apple and Google copied)](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2018/04/where-world-warning-letters-address-geolocation-coppa-coverage)
- Amendments were published in the Federal Register on **22 Apr 2025**, effective **23 Jun 2025**, with most operators given until **22 Apr 2026** to comply. — [The Toy Association](https://www.toyassociation.org/PressRoom2/News/2026-News/updated-coppa-rule-requirements-take-effect-april-22.aspx)
- Key new obligations: (1) **separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing a child's data to third parties**, unless the disclosure is "integral" to the service. The FTC indicated advertising, monetary consideration or AI training do not qualify. (2) A written, posted **data-retention policy** with purposes, business need and deletion timeframe; indefinite retention is banned. (3) A written **information security program** with annual risk assessment. (4) A broader "personal information" definition (biometrics, government IDs) and a new "**mixed audience**" category, where operators must determine whether a user is a child before collecting data. — [Davis Polk client update, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)
- FTC policy statement of **25 Feb 2026**: no enforcement of parental consent for data collected solely for age verification, under conditions. This does not cover services primarily directed to children, which must treat all users as children. — [FTC press release, Feb 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/02/ftc-issues-coppa-policy-statement-incentivize-use-age-verification-technologies-protect-children); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)

**State App Store Accountability Acts**
- **Texas SB 2420:** scheduled for 1 Jan 2026 and enjoined by a federal district court in Dec 2025. On **28 May 2026** the 5th Circuit stayed the injunction (administrative stay; on 5 Jun it allowed the law to remain in effect pending appeal). On **6 Jul 2026** the US Supreme Court declined to block it; the merits are still being litigated. Developers must assess and assign age ratings to apps **and individual in-app purchases**, and the law covers age verification and parental consent for downloads and IAP. — [Troutman, Dec 2025](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/12/federal-district-court-grants-preliminary-injunction-of-texas-app-store-accountability-act/); [Wiley alert, 8 Jun 2026](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Key-Developments-With-State-App-Store-Accountability-Acts-as-Texas-Act-Takes-Effect); [InfoLawGroup, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2026/7/7/supreme-court-clears-the-way-texass-app-store-accountability-act-is-now-enforceable); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251111-texas-targets-app-stores-with-new-accountability-law)
- Apple (3 Jun 2026, "Update for Apps Distributed in Texas"): new Texas Apple Accounts are subject to the law from **4 Jun 2026**, with age assurance and parental consent for downloads, IAP and **significant app changes**. Parents can revoke consent, and developers can get a server notification. Developers use the Declared Age Range API and the PermissionKit Significant Change API, and "developers are responsible for determining what counts as a significant change". — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/)
- Google: the Texas law is in effect after the appeals-court stay. Google offers the **Play Age Signals API (beta)** but does not require its use. SB 2420 may require age ratings on in-app products (SKUs). Developers flag significant changes on the Age Signals page in Play Console. Google "will not revoke app access or disable apps based on a parent denying approval". Google plans no change to country targeting; developers may geo-restrict. — [Play Console Help: Changes to Google Play for app store bills in US states](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691?hl=en)
- **Utah:** compliance deadline extended to **6 May 2027** (H.B. 498), AG enforcement removed, private right of action is the only enforcement. **Louisiana:** delayed from 1 Jul 2026 to **1 Jul 2027** (H.B. 977); developers may rely on app-store age and consent signals. **Alabama:** new law effective **1 Jan 2027**. "Significant change" in Utah excludes routine UI updates but **includes new in-app purchases or ads**. — [Wiley alert, 8 Jun 2026](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Key-Developments-With-State-App-Store-Accountability-Acts-as-Texas-Act-Takes-Effect); [FPF comparison, 3 Jun 2026](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/)
- Conflict: older coverage gave Utah 7 May 2026 and Louisiana 1 Jul 2026, the original dates. The June 2026 Wiley alert reports the 2026 amendments delaying both, and I treat Wiley as current. — [9to5Mac, Oct 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/08/apple-announces-new-requirements-for-apps-available-in-texas/); [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Key-Developments-With-State-App-Store-Accountability-Acts-as-Texas-Act-Takes-Effect)
- **California AB 1043 (Digital Age Assurance Act):** signed 13 Oct 2025, operative **1 Jan 2027**. A developer that receives an OS or app-store age-bracket signal is deemed to have actual knowledge of the age range. It must not request more than necessary or share the signal for unrelated purposes. Apps updated on or after 1 Jan 2026 and downloaded before 1 Jan 2027 must request a signal by **1 Jul 2027**. Penalties are up to $2,500 (negligent) or $7,500 (intentional) per affected child, enforced by the AG with no private right of action. A follow-on bill (AB 1856) is reported but unverified. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-introduces-new-age-verification-requirements-for-software-applications); [Alston & Bird](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/california-enacts-digital-age-verification-law/)

### Inferences
- **Lowest-risk option for this winter: do not distribute in the US** (and possibly other non-EU countries) at first. Both stores allow choosing countries, and Google explicitly allows developers to geo-restrict. That removes COPPA and the US state laws.
- If the US is included: with **no ads, no analytics and no network calls**, the app collects no "personal information", so COPPA's consent duties do not apply. The developer must still answer the age-rating questionnaires accurately (Texas/Utah liability for misleading ratings). With **ads**, separate verifiable parental consent for disclosure to ad networks is a real obstacle for a hobby developer.
- Adding ads or IAP later may count as a "significant change" (Utah wording), which would trigger parental re-consent flows via the stores.

### Gaps
- The legal status of the Texas litigation after July 2026 was not checked.
- Whether contextual ads to under-13s rely on COPPA's "support for internal operations" exception after the 2025 amendments was not verified.

## 6. EU rules beyond GDPR: DSA Art. 28 minors guidelines, DSA trader status in app stores, in-game currencies/loot boxes (CPC key principles)

### Takeaway
The Commission's **Art. 28 DSA guidelines on protecting minors (14 Jul 2025)** are non-binding but are the Commission's compliance benchmark. They apply to *online platforms* and exclude micro and small enterprises, so they do not bind a solo developer's offline game. They are still a good design checklist. The practical DSA issue is **trader status**: app stores must collect it, and a developer who monetizes (e.g., with ads) is likely a "trader", with address, phone and email **shown publicly** in the EU. The CPC Network's **7 key principles on in-game virtual currencies (21 Mar 2025)** matter only if real money is involved.

### Cited Findings
- On **14 Jul 2025** the Commission published guidelines on protecting minors under the DSA, together with an age-verification app prototype. They cover online platforms accessible to minors, **"with the exception of micro and small enterprises"**. Following them is voluntary and "does not automatically guarantee compliance". The Commission will use them to assess compliance with **Art. 28(1)**. Measures include disabling addictive features (streaks, read receipts, autoplay, push notifications), private-by-default accounts, block and mute tools, and age verification for adult content or gambling and age estimation otherwise. — [EC: Guidelines under the DSA](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/dsa-guidelines); [EC news, 14 Jul 2025](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-presents-guidelines-and-age-verification-app-prototype-safer-online-space-children); [Press release IP/25/1820](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_25_1820/IP_25_1820_EN.pdf)
- The guidelines call for protecting minors from exploitative commercial practices, **including virtual currencies and loot boxes**, so that they are not exposed to practices that exploit their lack of commercial literacy. Where a third party performs age assurance, the platform remains responsible. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/fra/news-information/protecting-minors-in-the-digital-space); [NatLawReview](https://www.natlawreview.com/article/european-commission-issues-guidelines-protection-minors)
- **Trader status (DSA):** Apple requires a trader-status declaration in App Store Connect for EU distribution. Factors include whether the app earns money, whether the developer advertises, VAT registration, and a connection to their profession. Traders must provide an address, phone and email for **public display** on EU product pages. Individuals must submit their contact details themselves (organizations often use their D-U-N-S data). Non-traders are flagged to EU customers as outside EU consumer law. Apps without trader status were removed from EU storefronts after **17 Feb 2025** (over 135,000 apps per Appfigures). — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/?p=55860); [uScreen guide to the App Store Connect DSA fields](https://help.uscreen.tv/en/articles/10197111-how-to-complete-digital-services-act-dsa-compliance-in-app-store-connect); [GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/apple_suspends_over_135000_apps_across_eu_app_stores-amp-66619.php); [heise](https://heise.de/-10287701)
- Google Play: a personal account that monetizes has its full address shown publicly (section 1). Google's DSA redress page refers to traders suspended for failing identity verification. I found no Play Console page describing the trader declaration itself. — [Play Console Help: required information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312?hl=en); [Google DSA redress options](https://support.google.com/european-union-digital-services-act-redress-options/answer/13535501?hl=en)
- **CPC Network key principles on in-game virtual currencies (21 Mar 2025), non-binding.** The 7 principles: clear prices with the **real-money equivalent** of virtual currency and items; no designs that obscure costs; no forcing players to buy unwanted currency; clear pre-contract information; respect for withdrawal rights; fair, plain-language terms; account for vulnerabilities, **including children**. They were issued with an enforcement action against Star Stable (a children's game) over direct appeals to children and time pressure. — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/cpcn-announces-virtual-currency-consumer-protection-guidelines/); [Lewis Silkin, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/03/26/eu-bodies-act-against-gaming-companies-to-protect-children-from-in-game-purchase-102k658); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/level-up-compliance-video-games-industry-in-the-spotlight-again-for-consumer-law-compliance)
- Store-level equivalents: Google bans "failing to distinguish virtual game currency from real-money purchases" in kids' apps. Apple counts **loot boxes** as a 9+ descriptor. — [Families Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335); [Apple age ratings](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/age-ratings)

### Inferences
- An offline single-player game with no user-generated content, chat or sharing is probably **not an "online platform"** under the DSA. Combined with the micro/small enterprise exclusion, the Art. 28 guidelines are not legally binding here. Following them anyway (no streak pressure, no push notifications, no dark patterns) helps with store review and parents' trust.
- **Without monetization**, the developer can likely declare "not a trader" and keep the home address private. **With ads**, trader status (address, phone and email shown in the EU) is likely. This is a practical reason to start ad-free.
- "Gold" and the armory are earned in-game only, with no real money. The CPC principles then mostly don't apply. Never add purchasable currency or loot boxes for this audience.

### Gaps
- Whether ad income alone makes an individual a "trader" under DSA Art. 30 has no official answer; it is a case-by-case test.
- The EU Digital Fairness Act proposal (expected to address loot boxes and virtual currencies) was not researched, and its status in late 2026 is unknown.

## 7. Taxes/business basics in Poland for an individual earning from in-app ads or sales

### Takeaway
Since 1 Jan 2026 the **działalność nierejestrowana** (unregistered activity) limit is **quarterly: 10 813,50 zł** (225% of the 4806 zł minimum wage). Above it, the activity must be registered in CEIDG within 7 days. Income goes on **PIT-36** at the tax scale, counted when actually received, with a simple sales record. For store payouts, Apple requires a US tax form (W-8BEN for non-US individuals) under its Paid Apps Agreement. A **free app with no IAP and no ads earns nothing and triggers none of this.**

### Cited Findings
- Only a natural person may run działalność nierejestrowana. The 2026 limit is set **quarterly: 10 813,50 zł = 225% of the minimum wage (4806 zł)**. Revenue counts when due (invoiced or billed), even if not yet paid. After exceeding the limit, the activity becomes business activity and must be registered in **CEIDG within 7 days**. Tax: annual **PIT-36**, "działalność nierejestrowana" row, at the **skala podatkowa**. Taxable revenue is the amount actually received, documented costs are deductible, there are no monthly advances, and the deadline is **30 April**. A simplified sales record is required, and a bill or invoice must be issued on the customer's request. VAT exemption applies if prior-year sales were ≤ **240 000 zł**, with listed exceptions. The tax identifier is usually PESEL, with NIP needed in some cases such as VAT registration. Minors aged 13–18 can run it but need a legal guardian's consent for contracts. The page's publication date is 2021, but it reflects the 2026 rules. — [biznes.gov.pl: Działalność nierejestrowana](https://www.biznes.gov.pl/pl/portal/00115)
- Labour offices confirm the 2026 change from a monthly to a quarterly limit. For registered unemployed people, the monthly limit is 2403 zł (50% of minimum wage). — [PUP Sosnowiec](https://sosnowiec.praca.gov.pl/strona-glowna/-/asset_publisher/Qat7ebECUfDp/content/dzialalnosc-nierejestrowana-nowe-zasady-od-2026-roku-); [biznes.gov.pl](https://www.biznes.gov.pl/pl/portal/00115)
- Apple: all developers must complete a US tax form for the Paid Apps Agreement. Non-US developers may need W-8BEN, W-8BEN-E or W-8ECI, and App Store Connect asks questions to choose the form. A submitted form can't be edited in App Store Connect. — [App Store Connect Help: Provide tax information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/provide-tax-information/)
- Forum reports, not official and possibly outdated, say Apple Finance treated App Store sales by non-US developers as not subject to US withholding, so the treaty part of W-8BEN was not needed. — [Apple Developer Forums thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/708842)

### Inferences
- Checklist: (1) start **free with no ads**, so there is no income and no tax obligations; (2) if ads are added, the account holder (an adult parent) earns the revenue, and up to 10 813,50 zł per quarter can fall under działalność nierejestrowana, filed on PIT-36; (3) keep a simple income record; (4) fill in the US tax form in each store's payout settings.
- Payouts typically come from Google's and Apple's foreign entities (B2B), so VAT treatment (possible VAT-UE registration for services to EU businesses) should be confirmed with **Krajowa Informacja Skarbowa** before the first payout.

### Gaps
- The other condition for działalność nierejestrowana (no registered business in the previous 60 months, per Prawo przedsiębiorców Art. 5) was not confirmed in the page extract.
- Whether ad revenue from Google AdMob fits działalność nierejestrowana, and how VAT applies to it, was not found on podatki.gov.pl or biznes.gov.pl. An individual tax interpretation or a KIS call is recommended.
- AdMob or Google Play payout tax-form requirements (W-8BEN in AdSense/AdMob payments) were not verified on Google's pages.
- The Poland–US tax treaty position on app income was not researched.
